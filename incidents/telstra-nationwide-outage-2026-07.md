Telstra suffered a nationwide mobile network outage beginning around 4:30am AEST on July 8, 2026, fully resolved by 4pm the same day. The company attributed the outage to a software defect in time-keeping servers at its Sydney and Melbourne data centres, which disrupted time synchronization across the network — mobile networks depend on precise timing distributed from a GNSS-derived reference clock through boundary clocks down to individual cell sites.

The disruption caused intermittent mobile calls and data sessions nationwide, affecting not only Telstra's own subscribers but also MVNOs riding its network, including Boost, Aldi Mobile, Tangerine, Belong, and Everyday Mobile. Potentially over 25 million people experienced signal issues.

Beyond consumer connectivity, the outage blocked hundreds of Triple Zero (emergency) calls, prompting 333 welfare checks, and halted regional train services that depend on Telstra's mobile network for signaling or communications.
