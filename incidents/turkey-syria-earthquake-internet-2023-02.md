On February 6, 2023, a 7.8 magnitude earthquake struck southern Turkey near Gaziantep, followed hours later by a 7.7 magnitude quake in the same region. The earthquakes caused catastrophic destruction across southeastern Turkey and northwestern Syria, killing more than 50,000 people and displacing millions.

Internet and telecommunications infrastructure in the affected areas suffered severe disruption. Power grid failures in the earthquake zone took down cellular base stations and ISP equipment. Physical infrastructure—including fiber routes, data centers, and exchange points—was damaged or destroyed in the hardest-hit areas. Cloudflare Radar documented significant traffic drops across Turkish and Syrian networks in the affected regions.

The connectivity collapse complicated rescue and relief operations in the critical first 72 hours, as emergency responders, survivors, and aid organizations struggled to communicate and coordinate. Satellite-based connectivity (including Starlink terminals) was deployed to assist relief efforts. Internet service gradually recovered as generators were brought online and emergency network repairs were made, though some areas remained disrupted for weeks.
